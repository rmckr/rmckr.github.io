# Design notes

## Brief

A personal portfolio for Lukas Romacker, a computer science student in Ulm. Dark, modern, premium: soft surfaces, generous space, one accent. The hero is a poster: the brand mark in the page's largest type, the name directly beneath it, and one project card standing in the fold.

## Direction

A quiet, editorial page over one loud ground. Structure comes from scale contrast and the two objects that earn a boundary — the readout and the holo card — not from boxes around everything. The page borrows its rules from a black hole: a dark ground, the brand mark standing on it, and the full name set directly beneath it line.

- **Background** `#111217` — flat near-black, cool. The page itself is only this colour: no gradient, no texture, nothing fixed behind it. The hero carries one drawing over that ground: a hairline blueprint grid. It runs the whole fold and fades out at its foot, so the drawing dissolves into the ground instead of stopping at an edge.
- **Foreground** `#dfe5eb` — pale paper, not white. Headings, names, primary text.
- **Muted** — `foreground` at 64% over `background`. Body copy and secondary labels.
- **Surface** — `foreground` at 4%. The fill behind buttons and control tracks.
- **Line** / **line-strong** — `foreground` at 12% for hairlines, 20% for control outlines.
- **Accent** `#ef233c` — red, used *only* to accentuate: primary action, availability dot, wordmark tile, selection, focus ring, the projects cube's faces and the Skills card behind the current row. Never for structure, dividers, or body text, with one exception: the experience timeline's rule and year ticks are that section's spine.
- **White** `#ffffff` — text and hairlines on a red fill (`.btn-solid`, `::selection`, the projects cube's faces). Never body text.

## Surfaces

- `.readout` — a window cut into the page: one hairline over the page's own ground, opaque, so what scrolls up behind its rules is cropped by an edge rather than showing through. It carries no radius and no shadow of its own: those stay in the markup, so the year panel takes the page's arc and its own inset shadow. The page's readouts are that one window: the experience year, and GitHub's four totals with a window each. All of them take the page's arc at all four corners and the page's one recessed treatment — an inset shadow offset downward, so the dark sits at the crown and the foot stays clean. The year window is padded across only, so its top and bottom rules *are* the window's crop lines and the figures slide behind them; each stats window is padded on all four sides and holds one `display-lg` figure, its `label` name outside the window, so the four read as one instrument rather than four cards. The projects cube is the page's one cast shadow — `0 2.5rem 4rem -1.5rem` black at 85% — because its cards are the only objects that sit off the page plane.
- `.btn` — 0.625rem rounded action over the same semi-transparent fill, its label at `body` size, and its leading set to 1 rather than the step's 1.7, so the 2.875rem box sets the height and the label is centred in it rather than stacked in a line box; `.btn-solid` is the only red fill on the page and marks the one primary action of its section — About's CV, Contact's mail — and its fill is the same 92% cut as the holo card's face, because white on the bare accent is only 4.22:1. The one compact control that is not sized like an action — the contact section's copy button — keeps its own size and 0.5rem radius but takes the same fill and the same 1px press.
- The bar is one row across the full width of the page: the mark at the left, the section links and the controls grouped at the right. It is the page's ground lifted one `surface` step — the same 4% overlay every raised control uses, laid over the header's own fill so the band stays opaque — with one hairline under it and a 6px shadow dropped from that edge: full width, square, no blur, nothing inside it boxed, so content scrolls under a hard edge rather than a floating pane. The footer's bar is the same row in the other order — copyright, credit, back to top — so the credit holds the centre line.
- The current section is set in `foreground` and carries the bar's only rule: one 2px `accent` bar that slides between entries and lands exactly as wide as the word above it. In the hero, and anywhere no section holds the reading band, no link is current and the rule is absent. The controls are words rather than boxes — the language pair is two labels, the chosen one in ink, and the menu button is a label with a 40px hit area — so the bar carries one frame and no second surface.
- No pill shapes anywhere; dots stay circular because they are dots.
- The wordmark mark — a 2rem `accent` tile at 0.5rem radius holding two white slashes — is the same mark as the favicon in `index.html`: an inline SVG square (100 units, 25 radius) with the slashes scaled to 43.75% and centred, so the tab and the nav never disagree. The letters beside it are the same `RMCKR` wordmark as the hero and the footer, set in Anton 400 uppercase at `body` size in the bar.
- The portrait is the page's only photograph. It is a transparent cutout, so it sits directly on `background` — no card, no fill: a 4:5 crop at `object-position: center 14%`, in full colour, flush with the frame's sides and foot so the shoulders end at the rules rather than floating short of them, with all four corners at the frame's inner radius — 0.25rem, `rounded-sm` — so no corner bites into the border. Behind the cutout there is nothing at all — no floor, no gradient, no fill: the crop's own transparency is what the ground shows through, and the frame's top rule crosses an uninterrupted cutout. The frame is 4px `foreground` at 0.5rem, and deliberately too short: it starts about 3rem below the top of the head, with the photo pulled up through its top rule, so the crown breaks the outline instead of sitting inside a box. The name is not typeset under it — it is signed: a scanned mark, flush with the frame's left edge, hanging beneath the foot. The paper it was scanned on is deleted rather than covered: the ink survives only as the asset's alpha channel, and `.signature` masks that over `foreground`, so no colour is stored in the image, the ink is the page's own, and a token change moves the signature with the type. Its box carries the asset's ratio, so the mark takes its height from its width and cannot stretch. Nothing laps the frame — the plate is signed below its foot, not stuck onto it. No second frame, no fill inside, no portrait in the hero. The photograph's source master (`portrait.png`) and the signature's scan (`Signatur.png`) sit in `public/` beside the two files that are served, as those assets' only originals.

## Type

Outfit for everything and Anton for the wordmarks. One text family carries the labels, the prose and the display, so the page has one voice.

No section carries an eyebrow. The heading is the first thing a chapter says; the label voice is kept for data (nav, dates, counters, the address), never for a label above a heading.

- Hero header: the brand wordmark `//RMCKR` in Anton 400 — the slashes in `accent`, the letters in `foreground` — uppercase, one line, flush left at `clamp(4rem, 12vw, 9.5rem)` on 0.8 leading, inside a 56% left column whose right leaves the fold to the card. It is the only place the brand is set at display size. The full name follows immediately beneath it in Outfit 500, mixed case, at `clamp(1.25rem, 2.2vw, 2rem)` on `tight` leading: the mark is the handle and the name is the person, and the text face keeps the name from echoing the mark in the same voice. The accessible `<h1>` carries the same name for screen readers.
- One `.wordmark` utility carries the brand's voice — Anton 400, uppercase, −0.01em — so the hero's poster, the footer's index and the bar's mark are one treatment at three scales; each site adds only its size and the leading that size needs.
- Every chapter opens with the same deck: `.section-lead`, the `lead` step in `muted`, measured by the grid rather than by a guessed length — from `lg` it is exactly the left column, half the container's content width less the gutter, so the line under a heading reads the same in every section and ends a gutter short of the seam whether the deck sits inside that column or at the section's left; below the split it takes a 46ch reading measure. The measure resolves against `container-page`, which is the page's query container (`container-type: inline-size`), so it is cut from the real column and not from the viewport. The gap under a heading is 1.5rem everywhere; only Contact sets its own, because its deck opens the body block under the availability line rather than hanging from the heading.
- The hero has three sizes: the wordmark, its name, and `display-lg` for the statement; its link is `label`. Everything else on the page is one of five `--text-*` steps plus the `label` voice, and no component sets a size by hand except the hero's wordmark and its name line. Two values are set by hand and are deliberate: `.btn` sets its own leading to 1, because a control's height is its own box and a prose leading would only inflate the line box around a label flex already centres; and the experience year is `tracking-normal`, because its tabular digits must not be shaved sideways.

| token | size / leading | used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `label` | 12px / 1.4, 0.1em, Outfit 500, uppercase | nav, dates, availability, footer bar |
| `body` | 1.0625rem / 1.6 | prose, organisation lines, emails, social labels, nav actions, footer links |
| `lead` | 1.375rem / 1.4 | section decks, entry titles, the footer wordmark |
| `display` | clamp(2.5rem, 5.5vw, 5rem) / 1.04, −0.015em, 600 | section headings, mobile menu |
| `display-lg` | clamp(2.25rem, 5.2vw, 4rem) / 0.98, −0.02em, 600 | hero statement, skill group names, the experience year on mobile |
| `display-xl` | clamp(4.25rem, min(13vw, 20vh), 13rem) / 1.1, −0.02em, 700 | experience year |

Highlighted phrases in prose are `foreground` at medium weight. Every micro-label on the page is `label`, in Outfit.

## Layout

One gutter on an 84rem container. `--gutter` (fluid, 1.25 to 3.5rem) is the page's only inset: the air between the page's edge and its content, and the air on either side of the half wherever a section splits. Across a split the two columns therefore stand `--seam` apart, twice the gutter, so the edge, the seam and both body columns all step from one number. Every body section breaks at the half, and the page has one axis instead of four. Below `lg` every section stacks to one column. Grid is not the primitive: no section has more than two tracks, and a twelve-column grid only offers eleven more places to put the boundary, which is how the page ended up with four. The page is nothing but `background`. The hero is the only section with a texture of its own: the blueprint grid sits behind the content as an isolated layer (`isolate` plus `-z-10`), and nothing is drawn over it but a fade at the foot, where the grid dissolves into the ground. The hero is exactly one screen (`h-svh`) and spends it as two columns: the wordmark, the name and the statement sit flush left in a 56% column, vertically centred between the bar and the link out, and the right of the fold is the holo card's, which Projects takes over as the page scrolls. The name runs beneath the wordmark in the text face, mixed case, so the mark is never shown without the person it stands for and the name never competes with it; the statement follows at `display-lg` (22ch measure, so it breaks into a block rather than a line); and the fold closes at its bottom left with the mail action — the section's one primary — and the `label` link out, the portfolio, beside it. Nothing in the hero may exceed the fold. No portrait; one primary action and one link out, and the CV and GitHub links live in About and Contact. About puts a cutout portrait in the left third — a 4px foreground frame the head breaks, signed beneath its foot — and hangs the prose from the seam's gutter, on the same axis as every other body column, at one size (`body`), vertically centred against the plate's height, with the CV action — the one red fill in the section — ending that block, so neither column hangs at the bottom. The plate is deliberately narrower than the side it sits in, so the gap between the two is wider here than anywhere else on the page. Below `lg` it stacks — the plate capped at 18rem above full-measure prose — because half the container would leave the lead under 40 characters, and the centred column has nothing to centre against until the plate is taller than the text. Experience is one pinned scene rather than a stack: the chapter mark and heading hold the top of the left column, the description its foot, and the largest numeral on the page its centre, on the recessed readout. The entries carry no surface: each holds one step of the pinned scene, half a screen — short enough that two entries share the screen, and the type crossing the read line stays in the page's own ink, so the section's red remains the rule and its year ticks and nothing is put behind a word. The timeline reads as the accent moving down it one entry at a time. A 2px `accent` rule runs down the middle, and one scene-wide gradient at the top and bottom of the stage fades the rule and the entries as they cross those edges; each entry, one step tall and newest first, rides up the right half with a 2px `accent` tick at its centre. The numeral reads the tick at the read line: the date is interpolated between the two ticks bracketing the line, so a gap gets the speed of its real length, while the year itself only ever shows whole — it steps at the turn and holds in between — and it lands on a tick's end month as that tick crosses the viewport's middle. Two entries that share a year therefore change the entries while the numeral holds. Each period label carries the month where it is known (`10/2022 — 03/2026`), a year without a month ends that December, and an entry still running (`10/2026 — present`) ends this month. When two phases share an end month the numeral holds while the entries change, and each entry's own period names the span. The track has no tail and the section no vertical padding: a quarter screen of air sits above the first tick and below the last — half a viewport less half a step — so the first tick is centred as the stage pins and the stage releases the moment the last one reaches the middle. Below `sm` the header and description return to the flow above the track, the pinned numeral drops, and the spine moves to the content's left edge, the entries indented past it with their ticks crossing it — so a phone keeps the timeline but an entry is bare text led by its year at `display-lg`. Skills is a text index beside the holo card; Contact has no surface at all.

Projects sits between the hero and About: one card per project on a Y ring — four make the box, five a pentagon — never a row of panels. The section is pinned for a little over two screens inside a sticky stage, and the ring turns one face per card backwards over that runway, so each card enters from the right and the section ends on the last one instead of coming back to the first. The fold's card is the holo card: `--holo-card-width` and `--holo-card-height` cut both, its height derived from its width and capped by the pinned stage, and the faces are that same size, so the card that hangs in the fold is the card the ring turns: one element, one size, no morph. Narrower than the card, the column decides at 88% of it, so the face's perspective overhang still closes inside the gutter. Its lean carries its own `perspective(1600px)`, because the face it hangs in is flat: without it the rotation is only compressed into that plane and reads as a skew, and in the ring the lean is zero, so the function is identity. Faces are held out at half a card over tan(π/cards) plus a hair, so they stand apart with air between them; the back of the box never shows. Each face's cover sits inside the card's own padding with no frame and no fill: `object-contain` against the face, so a banner is shown whole and the surface around it is the mat rather than a border around the image. A cover is a 1280-wide WebP (10–50 KB where the screenshot is 0.1–4.7 MB); the wide screenshots stay beside them in `public/images/repos/` as the masters, unreferenced. The cover sits under the face's own foil, so the light crosses the project's image with the card instead of stopping at its edge. The box takes the section's right column and hangs from that column's own left edge — the body axis the heading above it sits on, `--seam` from the list's column — so the box and the list are one axis apart rather than each floating in its half. The face it shows stands a depth in front of the page, so the scene's perspective widens the card past its box: the ring is shifted right by that overhang once per layout, so the card the reader sees, not the box that holds it, starts on that edge. The left column carries what the box cannot say: the heading, the lead, and the list of the four projects, one row per project with no rule between them — the ordinal in `label`, the name the only thing that changes as the ring turns, and the colour set once on the row itself — `foreground` on the card the ring is showing, `muted` on the rest — so the ordinal and the name are one line and cannot disagree, and the list is the index on screen while the box never has to name itself. The list is the section's control: a row is a step to its card, and under a fine pointer the row comes up to `foreground` as one line, since the colour lives on the row rather than on either span — so there are no chevrons to place, and on a wide screen the list is centred on the box's height, so the two columns read across instead of hanging from a shared top. The card is the one path out: its title is a link to the repository, tabbable only on the face the ring is showing, so a card behind is never a tab stop. The scene keeps a centred perspective origin so the ring widens evenly rather than being thrown to one side. The box is the card's own width rather than the widest the column allows: nothing in the flight resizes, because width is the one property a frame of scroll would have to lay out again, so the box takes exactly what the card is. The accent is not one face's property: it belongs to whichever card faces the reader, so it fades from face to face through the turn and every card is red exactly while it is the one being read. Only the fold's card carries the foil, and only while it is in the fold — inside the box the accent is one flat fill on all four, so no card is a different red from its neighbour. The section opens on that card: the whole cube is lifted by a plain 2D transform (outside the scene's perspective, so nothing in flight is scaled or skewed) one screen above its place, with both axes measured from the fold's own geometry — the card lands on the hero's centre line with its painted right edge on the fold's right gutter (painted, because the front face stands a depth in front of the page and the scene's perspective widens the card past the box that holds it), so a wrapped lead or a narrower column cannot push it off either — and the first screen of scroll brings it down as the stage arrives. The horizontal offset is spent by the time the card is home, so it is the card that hangs from the fold's right edge, not the box: at rest the card keeps its column's left edge. The list moves the page rather than the box: a row scrolls to that card's place in the stage's own pinned length, measured from the stage element rather than from the viewport's height, so the first and last faces land exactly where the pin begins and ends, and a row pressed below `lg` writes the ring's index instead, because the scroll cannot turn the box there. Beside the names, one 2px `accent` rule the height of one body line travels down the list with the turn — from the first row's centre to the last one's, proportional to the runway rather than snapping row to row — so the column says how far round the box is before a name is read. It is the nav marker's cousin: one rule, no track, no fill, and it is dropped under reduced motion, where the box is a plain grid of all four. The same element is the hero card and the front face — nothing is swapped or duplicated. What the fold adds is the card's pose — its rest lean, the pointer lean, which tips toward the pointer, and the rock of its breath — and all three ease out with the flight, so the box's faces stay square. The section ends there: the accent stays in the card, and the cube is the last place the page draws it in three dimensions — after it the same face is only ever a background. Below `lg`, and under `prefers-reduced-motion`, there is no flight and no turn: the cards lie in a plain grid — one column below `sm`, two from it — the front one already lit. Each cover is a `line` frame: the repository's own banner where there is one, `object-contain`, so no banner is cut whatever its shape, and the project's name in the brand face where there is not. Beneath it the name and one specific line, and the name is the link out.

Skills is the page's tool register: the languages, tools and systems told as prose rather than as an index. Four entries in one column at every width and no rule between them, so the column is the four names in air rather than a table. Each row is split at the half between one thing and the other rather than between two texts: the entry's name at `display-lg` on one side and its tools on the other, and every second row mirrors (`lg:flex-row-reverse`) — its name right-aligned against its own side and its rail on that side — so the column alternates rather than repeating one row four times. There is no description at all: the four names are the section's whole prose. No rule, no mark of its own and no eyebrow above a name. The one chrome is a single card at the screen's full width — the cube's own red surface, edge to edge on the 1rem arc, measured against the client area rather than `100vw` so the scrollbar's width is never taken out of it and neither arc is cut — behind whichever row the pointer is on. It is one element that takes that row's box and travels to the next over 320ms, so the accent is handed over rather than cross-faded and never sits between two rows; a row takes it as the pointer enters and keeps it once the pointer has left, so the card follows the reader's cursor rather than the page's scroll. On it the rail leaves `muted` for the page's own ink at full strength: ink is 3.8:1 on the deep accent face, which clears AA for the `display-lg` names and 3:1 for the marks, which are not text — white's 4.8:1 is not needed and would be a second colour on the page. Off the card the marks sit at `muted` and 70%, and their own 200ms colour transition carries both changes. Each row closes with its tools as a still row: brand marks at 2rem, wrapping rather than clipping when the column cannot hold them on one line. The rail is decorative, so it is `aria-hidden` and the same skills, with their levels, follow as an `sr-only` list. The rail's label is the pointer's: the mark under it comes up to ink inside a 2px ink frame, and that mark's name with its level is carried beside the cursor on a small hairlined plate in the page's own ground, so it reads over the page and over the card alike. The rail never moves, so the mark the pointer is on cannot leave the word that names it.

GitHub is the account behind the four projects, and it is a register rather than a second projects list: the heading and its deck open the chapter, and the body splits at the half. Left is the account: the avatar as a 5rem mark on a `line` hairline at the wordmark tile's 0.5rem arc, the name at `lead` in the text face, the handle in the body face under it (the page's only handle, so it is never set as a `label` and never shouted into caps), the fetched bio as the section's only prose, and one link out to the profile in the hero's link-out voice: `label`, `muted`, coming to ink under the pointer, so no red enters the chapter and nothing in it is a card. Right is the account's four totals — stars, forks, pull requests and issues, the numbers the loader already fetches — as four readout windows in a 2×2, a `label` name outside each — indented to the window's own inner inset, so the name and the figure it labels share one left edge — and one `display-lg` figure inside it, set as the experience counter's own dials so the number lands rather than counts; each window on its own arc, hairline and recess, one figure to a window and no chart or icon anywhere. The name, handle and link are static, so the chapter is whole before the request resolves; each window holds its line empty until its figure lands, and a failed fetch keeps the four names in place, names the failure and offers one retry.

Contact is the page's quietest section and the only one with no surface of its own: `SectionHeader` is the heading alone; availability — the dot and its `label`, no box — opens the left column above the prose, and only then does the body split at the half — prose and the mail action left, address and channels right — divided by one vertical `line` hairline that sits on the seam and spans the body only — the heading row has no rule, and the section carries no horizontal rule at all. The address and its copy control open the right column; GitHub and LinkedIn follow as unruled rows with their marks, so nothing in the section is a card or a box. The section takes extra vertical padding above and below its content (`py-4 md:py-12` inside `Section`'s own), so it breathes without the body needing a fixed height.

The footer is the page's index rather than a sign-off, and it is the bar's mirror — the ground lifted the same `surface` step, with the same shadow thrown upward instead of down — so the page closes on a raised band: under the section's top hairline, the index itself: one two-column list of seven cells from `sm` — the full `//RMCKR` wordmark at `lead`, its slashes in `accent`, opening the first cell as a link back to the top, then the six chapters unnumbered at `body` — so the index is four rows, the last holding Contact alone, with no heading above it and none of Contact's links repeated. A bar closes the page in three tracks, the copyright left, the credit at the centre (`Built with`, a filled `accent` heart, `and`, the React mark) and back to top at the right, all at `label`; a full-width hairline over it separates the index from the bar without closing the footer, so the page still ends on its last line of type instead of a second outer rule. No display type and no second name: the hero carries the name, and the footer closes on the copyright.

## Motion
Interaction states move as transitions — never a keyframe — over `--ease-out` = `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`, or `--ease-in-out` = `cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)` for on-screen movement. The experience counter and the GitHub figures are the page's only springs: they ride `--ease-mech`, a `linear()` sampled damping.

- **Nav, changing section.** One 2px `accent` rule travels between entries and lands exactly as wide as the word it sits under. The position and the width both ride 200ms on `--ease-out`, so the rule stretches on its way and settles into the next label instead of teleporting. It is one element in two layers — the outer translates, the inner scales about its left edge from a 128px base — so the move touches neither layout nor the page. There is only ever one rule, so nothing cross-fades; the label it lands under brightens over the same 200ms, and at the hero, where no section is current, there is no rule at all.
- **Language switch.** The two labels cross-fade their colour in 200ms: the chosen one takes ink, the other steps back to `muted`. No box carries the choice, so the pair never reads as one control with a missing piece.
- Both carry `motion-reduce:transition-none`: the state stays, the move goes.
- **Menu, opening.** The menu is a full screen of the page's own ground, so it rises from under the bar rather than appearing over it: 8px below its place and transparent, settling to neither over 220ms on `--ease-out`, and it leaves by the same edge in the same time, so closing is opening backwards. The surface outlives the state by one exit beat and is `inert` with no pointer events the moment it is closed, so the page behind it is reachable while it fades and no timer stands between a tap and the page. `@starting-style` gives the freshly mounted surface its off edge without a frame of JS, and focus still lands on Close at once — the move never delays the keyboard. `prefers-reduced-motion` drops the transition and keeps the mount, so the menu is simply there.
- **Copy, confirming.** The address's copy control swaps its mark as a cross-fade rather than a frame: the copy icon steps back to 94% and out while the check comes up from 94%, both over 120ms on `--ease-out` and both in the same cell, so the button never changes size. Beside it a `label` `Copied!` comes up in the same 120ms beat at `muted`, its line's slot held in the row at rest so the address keeps the wrap it had; the words are hidden from the accessibility tree, because the polite `sr-only` region beside them is the one that announces. It is the page's one confirmation, so it moves once and settles: no bounce, no repeat.
- **Action and hover, answering.** The action's fill and edge take 200ms on `--ease-out` — the same colour beat the nav and the language pair use — and its 1px press rides 160ms, quicker than the colour, so the button answers the thumb before the eye has to notice it move. Hover on the hero's second link, the footer's index and back-to-top, and Contact's rows takes the same 200ms `[color]` transition as the nav; the copy control eases its fill as well. Colour only, deliberately: `transition-colors` would drag `outline-color` into the beat and fade the focus ring. `prefers-reduced-motion` gives the press without a transition and still settles the colours.
- **Page, scrolling.** The wheel is smoothed by Lenis, which writes the real scroll position each frame: every drawing on the page — the card's flight, the ring's angle, the year's dial — keeps reading the document rather than a proxy, and nothing about the page's geometry changes. No snapping, because a snap would pull the document under drawings that are already watching it. Native anchor behaviour is left alone unless a link asks for a paced ride with `data-scroll-duration`; those are taken over, eased on a cubic in-out and landed under the bar, and they stay interruptible, so a wheel gesture takes the page back mid-flight. A landing is the section's own top and nothing else: the page sets no `scroll-padding-top` and no scripted offset, so a paced ride, a plain anchor and a focus scroll all stop on the same pixel — the chapter begins under the bar. Under reduced motion Lenis runs 1:1 and a paced link is an instant jump: the same landing, no ride.
- **Experience counter, counting.** The numeral is driven directly by scroll, interpolated between the end months of the ticks on the rule, so an irregular gap gets its own speed and every month in it passes. It is four dials, one per digit: the values stacked in a window exactly one line tall (`1lh`, `overflow: hidden`), the last value first, so a step back in time travels up with the page and a `0 → 9` step spins that digit's whole dial. Only the reel's `translate` moves, by `calc(var(--d) * -1lh)` where `--d` is the row. The window is deliberately tight: a figure's ink overshoots its line box, so the crop shaves it at both edges the way a machined counter window would. The dials are tracked normal and tabular, so a change moves only the digits that change and no digit is shaved sideways. The scroll handler writes `--d` on the reels directly and nothing is animated in JS: the geometry, the spring and the reduced-motion snap are the `dial` and `dial-reel` utilities. The reel rides `--ease-mech`, a damped spring sampled as `linear()`: it overshoots by 4.6% and settles, so a value lands in its detent rather than gliding to it, over 280ms (`--dial-ms` overrides it). The numeral steps whole years only, never a fraction of one, so a run of entries that share a year holds it still.
- **GitHub figures, landing.** The four totals are the experience counter's own dials, one per digit, so a figure does not tween through values: it spins to its number and lands in the detent. Nothing is animated in JS — React writes each digit's row as `--d`, and `dial`/`dial-reel` supply the window, the spring and the reduced-motion snap, exactly as they do for the year. The rows run `0` to `9` down the window, so every digit starts at zero and travels up to its own number. One `IntersectionObserver` per mount starts them the first time the grid is half on screen and is then disconnected, so the figures move once; the readout window already holds its line, so a figure gaining a digit moves neither its neighbours nor the page. Under reduced motion nothing is observed and nothing spins: the figures land at their values, and a window with no value yet stays empty rather than showing a zero.
- **Holo card, leaning.** Pointer position is written straight to two registered numbers on the wrapper — `--ratio-x` and `--ratio-y`, −1 at an edge and 0 at the centre — which the lean's `transform` (±18° and ±12° about the rest pose) and every foil gradient read. A frame loop chases the numbers toward the pointer by 16% a frame and stops once settled, so entering and leaving both ease rather than snapping, and nothing runs between moves. The pose leans the card away from the reader at the top edge and at the left edge — the face turns up and to the left — and the pointer pushes the side it is over further back while the opposite side comes toward the reader, so the card is pressed into the page rather than lifted at the cursor. Touch and `prefers-reduced-motion` never set them, so the holo card keeps its rest pose and its resting foil.
- **Holo card, foil.** The foil is white light on the deep `accent` face: a white lamp above the card and right of centre, a broader wash corner to corner, a soft specular streak and a faint dot grid. Every layer blends `screen` at a fixed strength (0.40) straight onto the face, which supplies the hue: the sheen runs from the card's own red to a pale highlight, and nothing is laid on as an opaque film. So one light field crosses the whole face, the project's cover included — the image sits beneath the foil rather than beside it, and the heading, counter and title are lifted above it, so only the image takes the light and the words stay crisp. Angle and lamp are set once per face (`FRONT_LIGHT` in `Projects.tsx`), so the fold's card and the ring's faces are lit by the same lamp. The lamp and the stops move together, driven by the pointer ratios and the breathing sway — hover changes nothing.
- **Holo card, breathing.** The card group rocks 1.2° either side of upright over nine seconds on `linear` between eight stops that trace a sine, so the swing keeps its speed through the middle and only eases at the turn instead of stalling at both ends. Its keyframes animate only the registered `--sway` (−1 → 1), which the foil's stops read, so the light shifts across the face as the card rocks, and which `cube-card` spends as the card's own `rotate`, weighted by the flight — so the rock is full in the fold and square by the time the card is the ring's front face, settling with the pose instead of being cut off when the fold's card docks. The angle is `rotate` rather than `transform`, so it composes over the pointer lean and the flight instead of replacing them. `motion-reduce:animate-none` holds it level.
- **Projects cube, turning.** The ring's rotation is driven straight by scroll — no easing, because the reader's hand is the easing — one face per card, backwards, over the section's runway — three for the four-card box — so the turn stops when the scrolling stops and the box ends on its last card. Only the transform moves, and every face carries `backface-visibility: hidden`, so the box never has to fade a face. The accent rides the facing angle, not the turn: each face's `--lit` is the cosine of its angle to the reader, faded in over the last ~45°, so the red is handed over through the turn and no face is ever lit twice. The project index adds no second driver: a row is a scroll to that face's place in the runway, and below `lg`, where the scroll cannot turn the box, it writes the ring's index instead, so the box still turns on one number. Its 2px rule rides that same turn, travelling from the first row's centre to the last one's at a constant rate, so the index's rule and the box's angle are never two states of one fact.
- **Accent, changing entry.** In Skills the card's face is a background rather than an object: one card takes the box of the row under the pointer and travels to it over 320ms on `--ease-in-out`. It never fades — a card that faded would read as two — so the red is on one row at a time and a handover is a move rather than a teleport. The box is written once per row change and the element carries the transition — no frame loop paints it, so the card sits in the page's own flow and the page's own scroll carries it. The marks take their ink as soon as the card sets off — the lit rail is the same state the card follows — over the 200ms colour transition the rail already carries, so the ink and the move overlap instead of the ink arriving late; `prefers-reduced-motion` drops the card's move so only the ink transitions. The card is the column's, not the text's measure: it runs the screen's full width, edge to edge, measured against the client area rather than `100vw`, so neither arc is cut by the scrollbar. Experience carries no card at all: its scenes are the rule, the year readout and the entries themselves, and its type stays in the page's own ink from the first tick to the last.
- **Hero card, landing.** The card's lift is `--p`, the first screen of scroll: it moves in the page's plane from the hero's right gutter, where the fold's card hangs, into the ring's front face on the right column's left edge, its horizontal offset solved once per layout from the card's painted edge in the fold's own pose, so the edge that comes to rest on the gutter is the one the reader sees rather than the layout box behind it, and its pose — rest lean, pointer lean and breath — is multiplied by the same value, so it arrives square and the box's faces stay true; its foil fades with the same value, so the fold's card loses its light as it becomes one of the box's flat faces. The flight is measured against the section's own top rather than the viewport's height, so the card is on its face at the stage's first pixel, and its variables are written on that one element rather than the section, so a frame of flight repaints the card and not the stage around it. Its width is not part of the flight at all: the card is 23rem in the fold and 23rem in the ring, so the move is a transform and a pose with no layout behind it — the card is the face from the first pixel, and the ring is that card's size. Under reduced motion there is no card in the fold at all: the card is already home, so the hero's right column stays empty rather than holding a still card, and colour and layout survive while movement does not.
- **Availability dot, pulsing.** The dot beside availability is a ring that leaves it and fades: opacity 0.55 → 0 while scaling 1 → 2.2 over the first 70% of a 2.4s cycle, the remaining 30% dark, on `--ease-out`. The dot under it never moves, so neither the availability row nor the heading row can shift with the beat, and the ring is the dot's own `accent`, so red gains no new site. `motion-reduce:animate-none` parks the ring at scale 1 under the dot, where it is invisible.
- **Skills rail, marking.** The rail is still: the page has no drift. The mark under the pointer moves from `muted` to `foreground` in 200ms while its 2px ink frame fades in, and its name and level fade in over 120ms at the pointer rather than at the mark, so nothing travels to reach it. The row that owns the card takes its marks to full strength as the card sets off, and their own colour transition carries both changes. `prefers-reduced-motion` changes nothing here, because there is no movement to drop.
- **Experience edges.** One background gradient covers the pinned scene's top and bottom 14%, above the entries and below the left column, so the rule and every entry dissolve into the page as they cross those edges instead of being cut by the viewport. It is a still layer, not a transition; the header, description and year sit above it and never fade.
- The bar itself does not animate at all. It is the same band of ground at every scroll position — same height, same hairline — so nothing about the page's frame moves while the page does.

Beyond those states, the page moves in two registers and nothing else. The ambient loops are two CSS keyframes — the holo card's breath and the availability dot's pulse — and the card is the only thing moving in the fold, while the pulse lives in Contact, where availability is stated. The paced anchor rides carry a link to its section over its own duration. Both registers hold still under `prefers-reduced-motion`: the loops park and the rides land at once.

From `src/i18n` and `src/data` only. No invented facts. The GitHub chapter reads the account's four totals live through `loadGitHub` — stars and forks summed from the non-fork repositories, pull requests and issues from GitHub's own search counts — and caches them for ten minutes, so an unauthenticated visit spends its rate limit on one set of requests rather than on every render. ## Implementation contract

Tokens live in `src/index.css` under `@theme`; components use them through Tailwind classes (`bg-background`, `bg-surface`, `text-foreground`, `text-muted`, `border-line`, `text-accent`) and never through new hex values.

| token | value | role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `background` | `#111217` | page |
| `foreground` | `#dfe5eb` | headings, primary text |
| `surface` | `foreground` 4% | bar band, menu rows, control fills |
| `muted` | `foreground` 64% over `background` | body, secondary text |
| `line` | `foreground` 12% | readout borders, dividers |
| `line-strong` | `foreground` 20% | control outlines |
| `accent` | `#ef233c` | see below |

Pure white needs no token: Tailwind's built-in `white` is `#ffffff`, so `text-white` and `bg-white/10` all resolve without adding a palette entry.

Skills introduces no colour of its own. The four names are the page's ink on the page's own ground; the section's one colour is the page's accent, and it sits behind the row under the pointer rather than on any text. That face is the cube's own `.holo-card-face` fill, and the row's type and marks stay `foreground` — the page's ink, never white: ink measures 3.8:1 on the deep accent face, which clears AA for the `display-lg` names and clears 3:1 for the marks, which are not text. The rail's marks are `muted` at 70% off the card and `foreground` at full strength on it, both carried by their own 200ms colour transition; the tooltip is `foreground` with its level at `muted`, on a plate of `background`, so its 12px label never sits directly on the red. No second red, no white on the page, no tier blocks.

Everything else is derived from these tokens, never typed: every button rests on `surface` at 50% rather than on white, so its fill is genuinely translucent and the page's ground shows through it — 80% was tested and reads as opaque on a `#111217` page; white fills only at 6% on hover; the readout is the ground itself, opaque, so nothing shows through it; `.btn`'s hover border is `line` mixed 90% with `foreground`; `.btn-solid`'s hover is `accent` mixed 84% with `background`, a step deeper than its rest. The hero's blueprint is the page's only texture.

The hero's blueprint is `Blueprint.tsx` plus the `.blueprint` utility: a 4rem minor and 20rem major hairline grid in `foreground` at 4% and 8%, and nothing else. It is static and sits on the plain ground.

Red appears in exactly fourteen places: `.btn-solid` fill, availability dot, wordmark tile, `::selection`, `:focus-visible`, the 2px rule under the nav's current entry, the mail address's hover rule, the experience rule with its year ticks, the footer's heart, the hero and footer wordmarks' slashes, the projects cube's cards, the projects index's progress rule, and the Skills card behind the current row. Nowhere else — not section borders, not readout dividers, not the rail's marks, not body text, and not the hero's name: all of its type is `foreground`.

Radii: readout 1rem, holo face 1rem, nested image 0.25rem, `.btn` 0.625rem (0.5rem on the compact copy button), wordmark tile 0.5rem, icon tiles 0.375rem, the favicon 25/100 of its tile. No pill radii; circles only for dots.

Type: Outfit 400/500/600/700 and Anton 400 only. Display text is weight 600 (the year 700) with −0.015…−0.02em tracking; the wordmarks are Anton 400; highlighted phrases are `foreground` at medium weight; micro-labels use `.label`. Sizes, leading and display tracking live in the `--text-*` tokens; the one leading a component sets by hand is `.btn`'s 1, because a control's height is its box.

The hero has no light layer. Its fold is the page's `background` with the blueprint grid, and the only colour on it comes from the card's own accent face; red does not light the fold.

## Working across chats

New chats do not inherit this conversation, so the contract has to live in files. Every chat should read `AGENTS.md` and this file before touching UI. A prompt that holds the line:

> Read AGENTS.md and DESIGN.md, then <task>. Keep the existing design language: tokens only, red for accentuation only, no pill shapes, motion only as listed under Motion.

- One section or one concern per chat, so diffs stay reviewable and the language is not re-invented in three places at once.

- Commit a checkpoint before starting a new chat, so the next agent has a diff to respect.
